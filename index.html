Operating principles

AI is a component, not the entire architecture.

I use models where judgment, extraction, or synthesis helps — and deterministic systems where consistency, validation, cost, and control matter more.

Problem firstStart with the bottleneck and success condition, then choose the technology.
Evidence before confidenceTrace important conclusions back to source material, tests, logs, or durable artifacts.
Cheap before expensiveUse structure, search, rules, caching, and deterministic processing before broad LLM calls.
Preserve the sourceKeep original evidence durable so derived intelligence can improve without recreating it.
Human gates for consequential choicesMoney, credentials, permissions, privacy exposure, destructive actions, and major product choices stay explicit.
Design for restartChats fill up, APIs fail, runners disappear. Systems should resume instead of starting over.
Direction

Domain judgment first. Technical leverage second.

Find an expensive, repetitive, information-heavy problem. Understand the workflow better than the automation does. Then build the smallest system that materially improves it.

My long-term direction is practical AI: focused systems with clear value, controlled costs, traceable evidence, and a clear human decision boundary.

Background

Real-world judgment behind the systems.

My path into AI is non-traditional. I spent years investigating fraud and insurance claims, reviewing evidence, applying rules to incomplete information, documenting decisions, and working in deadline-driven regulated environments.

That background is why I care about provenance, edge cases, review gates, contradictions, and whether a system can explain what it did — not just whether a model produced an answer.
